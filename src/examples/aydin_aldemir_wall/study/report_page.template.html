The tension tail — what solved and paper mean

Aydin's concrete cracks along a trilinear tension tail: linear to (εcr, ft), then down through knees at a1εcr and a2εcr, reaching zero at a3εcr. The shape constants a1, b1, b2 are the same for every specimen in his Table 1. How far the tail runs — a2 and a3 — is the axis.

solved — a2 and a3 are computed per strut so it dissipates exactly Gf = 75 N/m over its own length. A longer strut gets a shorter tail. That is what makes the model mesh-objective.

paper — a2 = 70, a3 = 360, the values he prints for this specimen, fitted at his grid size of 20 mm. This study runs at 50 mm, and transplanting a fitted tail across grid sizes breaks the energy identity: measured, it dissipates 5.26 × Gf. It buys +22.6% base shear, which is why the paper cells overshoot at 1.19 × and why the column is dropped rather than pursued.

The axis exists only for the two laws built on his trilinear backbone. crushing uses Concrete02, whose tail is bilinear and regularised differently, so crushing / paper cannot be built — three of the gaps are permanent.

Reading the bars and the charts

Bar length is the overshoot above the measured 963.6 kN; the oxide tick at the left edge is the measurement itself.

Each analysis carries two panes of the same data — the full run to its own reach, and the test range only, cut where the record stops. Both use one vertical scale, so the dashed oxide line at 963.6 kN sits at a single height throughout and the panes differ only in x.

The matrix is keyed on compression law × tension tail × bond, so any other parameter shadows: the newest run in a cell may be a mesh or damping variant standing where the baseline was. Each row therefore names what is non-default about the run actually shown — an amber chip such as mesh 25 means you are not looking at the cell's plain baseline.

Cells marked start-up spike peak below 0.1% drift, where a bond ring acts as a parallel load path. That force is real but it is not sustained resistance; each such row prints what it actually holds afterwards.
